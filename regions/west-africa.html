Regions / West Africa

West Africa

Food Culture

From Senegal to Nigeria, West African food is bold, generous and made to share. Meals often centre on a starchy staple served with a rich, spicy soup or stew. Large pots are cooked for the whole family, and at weddings and festivals no table is complete without a big tray of jollof rice.

Many of the region's flavours travelled across the Atlantic. Okra, black-eyed peas and one-pot rice cooking shaped dishes such as gumbo and jambalaya in the Americas.

Staple Ingredients

  • Grains: rice, millet, sorghum and fonio
  • Roots & tubers: yam, cassava and cocoyam
  • Plantains: fried as dodo or kelewele
  • Seeds & nuts: groundnuts and egusi (melon seeds)
  • Flavour: scotch bonnet, palm oil, ginger and dried fish

Cooking Techniques

  • Pounding: boiled yam or cassava is pounded into smooth fufu.
  • Stewing: tomato-and-pepper bases are cooked down slowly until rich.
  • Grilling: spiced suya skewers are cooked over open flames.
  • Smoking and drying: fish is preserved to add deep, savoury flavour.
  • Fermenting: locust beans become dawadawa, a powerful seasoning.

Signature Dishes

Also try: egusi soup, suya, waakye, thieboudienne and groundnut stew.
