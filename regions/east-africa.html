East Africa

Food Culture

East Africa stretches from the Ethiopian highlands to the Great Lakes and the Swahili coast, and its food is just as varied. In Ethiopia, meals are served on injera and eaten by hand. Ethiopia is also the birthplace of coffee, and the coffee ceremony is a cherished social ritual.

In Rwanda, Uganda and Kenya, everyday meals are built on beans, plantains (matoke), cassava and maize. On the Swahili coast, centuries of Indian Ocean trade brought cloves, cardamom and coconut into dishes like pilau and biryani.

Staple Ingredients

  • Grains: teff, maize (for ugali) and sorghum
  • Starches: green bananas (matoke), cassava and sweet potato
  • Greens: cassava leaves, sukuma wiki (collard greens) and spinach
  • Protein: beans, lentils, peanuts and lake fish like tilapia
  • Spices: berbere, mitmita, cardamom and cloves

Cooking Techniques

  • Fermenting: teff batter ferments for days to make tangy injera.
  • Slow stewing: Ethiopian wats begin with onions cooked down for a long time without oil.
  • Spiced butter: niter kibbeh is clarified butter infused with herbs and spices.
  • Pounding: cassava leaves are pounded for Rwandan isombe.
  • Steaming in leaves: matoke is wrapped in banana leaves and steamed until soft.

Signature Dishes

Also try: doro wat, ugali with sukuma wiki, nyama choma, Rolex (Ugandan egg roll) and Zanzibar pilau.
