North Africa

Food Culture

North African cooking, from Morocco and Algeria to Tunisia, Libya and Egypt, mixes Amazigh (Berber) traditions with Arab, Mediterranean, Andalusian and Ottoman influences. Meals are shared from a central dish, hospitality is sacred, and sweet mint tea is poured for every guest.

On Fridays, families across the Maghreb traditionally gather for a big plate of couscous. Couscous is so central to the region that it was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2020.

Staple Ingredients

  • Grains: semolina couscous, wheat bread and barley
  • Legumes: chickpeas, lentils and fava beans
  • Fruit: olives, dates, figs, apricots and lemons
  • Herbs: fresh mint, coriander and parsley
  • Spices: cumin, saffron, cinnamon, harissa and ras el hanout

Cooking Techniques

  • Slow braising: meat and vegetables cook gently for hours in a clay tagine.
  • Steaming: couscous is steamed two or three times over a simmering stew in a couscoussier.
  • Preserving: lemons are packed in salt for weeks until soft and tangy.
  • Spice blending: ras el hanout ("head of the shop") can contain over 20 spices.
  • Baking: flatbreads like khobz are baked in communal ovens.

Signature Dishes

Also try: couscous with seven vegetables, harira soup, shakshuka, Egyptian koshari and pastilla.
