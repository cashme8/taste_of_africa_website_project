Jollof Rice

The Story Behind the Dish

Jollof rice traces its roots to the Wolof people of the Senegambia region, where a similar dish, thieboudienne, is still a national favourite. It spread across West Africa and every country made it its own. Today it is cooked from Senegal to Cameroon.

The dish is famous for the friendly "Jollof Wars", a long-running debate (mostly between Nigeria and Ghana) over who makes it best. Nigerian cooks often use long-grain parboiled rice, while Ghanaians tend to prefer fragrant basmati. Everyone agrees on one thing: the prized smoky "party" flavour comes from letting the bottom of the pot lightly toast.

  • Origin: Senegambia, West Africa
  • Prep: 20 min
  • Cook: 50 min
  • Difficulty: Medium

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 large tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper (adjust to taste)
  • 2 medium onions
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 0.5 cup vegetable oil
  • 3 cups chicken stock
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

Show cooking steps
  1. Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth.
  2. Slice the second onion and fry it in the oil over medium heat until soft and golden.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and fry for 3–4 minutes until it darkens slightly.
  4. Pour in the blended pepper mix. Simmer uncovered for 20–25 minutes, stirring often, until the sauce thickens and the oil rises to the top.
  5. Add the stock, thyme, curry powder, bay leaves and salt. Bring to a boil.
  6. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then stir it into the pot.
  7. Cover tightly (a layer of foil under the lid helps) and steam on low heat for 25–30 minutes without stirring.
  8. For the smoky "party" flavour, turn up the heat for the last 2–3 minutes so the base gently toasts. Fluff and serve.
