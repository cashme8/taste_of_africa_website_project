Bunny Chow

The Story Behind the Dish

Bunny chow was born in Durban, South Africa, home to one of the largest Indian communities outside India. Their ancestors came to work on the sugar plantations in the 1800s. The idea was simple and clever: a hollowed-out loaf of white bread makes an edible container, so a hot curry can be carried and eaten anywhere without a plate.

Despite the name, there is no rabbit in it! One popular theory says the name comes from "bania", a word for the Indian merchant community. Today bunny chow is a proud symbol of Durban. The scooped-out bread "plug" is used to mop up the spicy, garam masala-scented gravy.

  • Origin: Durban, South Africa
  • Prep: 20 min
  • Cook: 1 hr
  • Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • 1 unsliced white loaf, cut into quarters
  • 800 g lamb or chicken, cut into chunks
  • 3 medium potatoes, cubed
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, grated
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tbsp Durban curry masala
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 curry leaves
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 cups water

Method

Show cooking steps
  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot. Add the cinnamon stick and curry leaves and let them sizzle for 30 seconds to bloom the spices.
  2. Add the onion and fry until golden, then stir in the ginger-garlic paste for 1 minute.
  3. Add the curry masala and a splash of water and cook for 1 minute so the spices don't burn.
  4. Add the meat and brown it on all sides, then stir in the grated tomatoes.
  5. Pour in the water, cover and simmer for 35 minutes.
  6. Add the potatoes and cook for 20 minutes more, until tender and the gravy has thickened. Stir in the garam masala.
  7. Hollow out each bread quarter, keeping the soft middle (the "virgin") to one side.
  8. Ladle the curry into the bread, put the bread lid back on top and serve with grated carrot salad.
