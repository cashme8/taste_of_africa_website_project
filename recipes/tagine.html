Chicken Tagine with Preserved Lemon & Olives

The Story Behind the Dish

"Tagine" is both the name of the dish and the clay pot it is cooked in. The pot's tall, conical lid catches rising steam, which runs back down into the stew. That means the meat can braise gently for hours with very little water, a clever idea for dry regions.

The dish has its roots in Amazigh (Berber) cooking in Morocco, and was later enriched by Arab, Andalusian and Ottoman influences. Classic tagines balance sweet and savoury: saffron, ginger and turmeric sit alongside salty preserved lemons and green olives. In Morocco, a tagine is placed in the centre of the table and eaten together, using bread to scoop up the sauce.

  • Origin: Morocco, North Africa
  • Prep: 20 min (+ marinating)
  • Cook: 1 hr 15 min
  • Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • 8 chicken thighs (bone-in)
  • 2 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 preserved lemon, rind only, sliced
  • 1 cup green olives
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 0.25 tsp saffron threads
  • 1 cup chicken stock
  • 0.5 cup fresh coriander and parsley, chopped

Method

Show cooking steps
  1. Soak the saffron in 2 tbsp warm water for 10 minutes.
  2. Marinate the chicken with the garlic, ginger, turmeric, cumin, saffron water, half the herbs and a pinch of salt for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a tagine or heavy pot. Spread the sliced onions over the base.
  4. Arrange the chicken on top of the onions and pour over any leftover marinade and the stock.
  5. Cover and cook on low heat for 45 minutes. Let it braise gently, adding a splash of water if it looks dry.
  6. Add the preserved lemon and olives. Cook uncovered for 15 minutes more so the sauce reduces and thickens.
  7. Scatter over the remaining herbs and serve with crusty bread or couscous.
