Injera

The Story Behind the Dish

Injera is the heart of every meal in Ethiopia and Eritrea. This large, spongy flatbread is made from teff, a tiny grain native to the Ethiopian highlands and farmed there for thousands of years. Its tangy flavour comes from fermenting the batter for several days.

Injera is spread across a shared platter and topped with stews (wats) such as spicy berbere chicken or lentil misir wat. Diners tear off pieces to scoop up the food. Hand-feeding a friend a mouthful, called gursha, is a traditional sign of love and respect.

  • Origin: Ethiopia & Eritrea, East Africa
  • Prep: 10 min (+ 2–3 days fermenting)
  • Cook: 3 min per injera
  • Difficulty: Medium

Ingredients

  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups lukewarm water
  • 0.5 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp oil (for the pan)

Tip: the batter ferments using wild yeast from the flour and air, so you don't need to add yeast.

Method

Show cooking steps
  1. Mix the teff flour and water in a large glass or ceramic bowl until smooth, like thin pancake batter.
  2. Cover loosely with a cloth and leave at room temperature to ferment for 2–3 days. It is ready when bubbly and pleasantly sour.
  3. Pour off any clear liquid on top, stir in the salt, and add a little water if the batter is too thick.
  4. Heat a large non-stick pan (or a traditional mitad) over medium heat and wipe it lightly with oil.
  5. Pour batter in a spiral from the outside in to make a thin, even round.
  6. When holes appear across the surface, cover and steam for 1–2 minutes. Never flip injera; it cooks on one side only.
  7. When the edges lift and the top is dry, slide it onto a clean cloth to cool. Repeat with the rest of the batter.
