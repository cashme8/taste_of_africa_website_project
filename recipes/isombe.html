Isombe

The Story Behind the Dish

Isombe is one of Rwanda's best-loved home dishes, also eaten in neighbouring Burundi and eastern DR Congo. It is made from young cassava leaves, which are pounded by hand in a wooden mortar, traditionally with a rhythmic beat that fills the compound.

Cassava leaves must always be cooked for a long time. Long cooking makes them safe to eat and turns them soft and silky. Rwandan cooks enrich the stew with eggplant, onions and peanut butter or palm oil, making a creamy, nutritious meal full of protein, iron and vitamins.

  • Origin: Rwanda, East Africa
  • Prep: 20 min
  • Cook: 1 hr 30 min
  • Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • 500 g cassava leaves (fresh, or frozen pounded)
  • 2 medium eggplants, diced
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 1 green bell pepper, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 2 cups fresh spinach, chopped
  • 4 tbsp natural peanut butter
  • 3 tbsp palm oil or vegetable oil
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

Show cooking steps
  1. If using fresh leaves, wash them well and pound them in a mortar (or pulse in a food processor) until finely crushed.
  2. Place the cassava leaves in a large pot with the water. Bring to a boil, then simmer uncovered for 45 minutes, stirring now and then.
  3. Add the eggplant, onion, bell pepper and garlic. Cook for 20 minutes more until the eggplant is very soft.
  4. Loosen the peanut butter with a ladle of the hot cooking liquid, then stir it into the pot.
  5. Add the spinach, palm oil and salt. Simmer for 15–20 minutes until thick and creamy.
  6. Taste, adjust the seasoning and serve hot with rice, ugali or boiled plantains.
